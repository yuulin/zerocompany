Star Wars Zero Company Beginner Guide: Positioning, Permadeath and Squad Rules That Matter

Category: Guides & Walkthroughs • Updated: August 2026
⚡ The Short Answer: Star Wars Zero Company is XCOM-style turn-based tactics from Bit Reactor — a studio built by former XCOM developers — wrapped in Clone Wars-era presentation with Mass Effect-style squad relationships and optional permadeath. The verified beginner truths: positioning and resource management decide fights, permanent losses are real if you enable permadeath, and the campaign is structured so no input timing ever decides an outcome.

The Design You're Walking Into

PC Gamer's coverage frames it precisely: a tactics game from Bit Reactor, a studio of former XCOM developers, co-developed with Respawn and published by EA — with permadeath as an optional rule and squad relationships that reviewers keep comparing to Mass Effect. The r/Games launch discussion's top framing: "more than just Star Wars XCOM — it feels like Mass Effect but with turn-based tactics and permadeath." Know the three pillars: positioning (cover, elevation, sightlines), resource management (gear, consumables, unit roster), and relationships (who lives together in your campaign story).

Permadeath: Decide Before Mission One

Permadeath is optional and it reframes every decision. Reviewers' shared observation: it "suddenly becomes much more unpleasant when the Clone Trooper you've spent ten missions building around is killed." Two honest postures: first campaign off — learn the tactical systems while the story bonds form; second campaign on — the intended tension, where extraction decisions and wound management carry real weight. Steam's Adjustable Difficulty and Save Anytime categories mean you can also save-scum as a middle path without shame.

Combat Fundamentals the Guides Agree On

Consolidated from the Into Indie Games ultimate guide and Hack the Minotaur's systems breakdown: positioning is the game — high ground and hard cover math outrank weapon choice in most engagements; economy is squad-wide — upgrades spent on a favorite are upgrades denied to the roster that must absorb losses; and action economy wins fights — planning full squad turns before committing the first unit is the habit that separates XCOM veterans from launches. The 12-specialization system (8 standard + 4 exotic classes) rewards drafting a roster with overlapping coverage rather than four copies of your favorite.

Where the Community Landed in Week One

The Fextralife wiki (79 pages within two days — including a loadout planner) centers its early meta on the Scoundrel as the strongest starting specialization and lists the exotic unit Cly Kullervo among top-tier units. Treat week-one consensus as provisional — balance patches during the launch window will move it — but if you want the community's current answer to 'who do I draft first,' that's it. Frequently Asked Directives

Is Star Wars Zero Company just XCOM with Star Wars skin? ▼
No — it's built by ex-XCOM developers, but squad relationships, permadeath stakes and cinematic presentation shift it toward 'Mass Effect with turn-based tactics,' per the launch consensus.
Does Star Wars Zero Company have permadeath? ▼
Yes, as an optional rule. Reviewers highlight how much it changes the emotional weight of squad losses.
Who makes Star Wars Zero Company? ▼
Bit Reactor (founded by former XCOM developers), co-developed with Respawn, published by EA. Set in the Clone Wars era.
How many classes are there? ▼
12 specializations — 8 standard classes (including Assault, Heavy, Sharpshooter and Scoundrel) plus 4 exotic classes.
